TITLE: Japan's "chase-out rooms": paid to do nothing
ALT TITLES: Why some Japanese workers get paid to sit in an empty room | The room Japanese companies use instead of firing you

DESCRIPTION:
Picture being moved to a tiny room with no computer and no work, and told to sit there until six.

In Japan, a dismissal is invalid unless the employer has an objectively reasonable reason that society would also accept (Labor Contracts Act, Article 16), so firing a full-time worker is very hard. Some companies have been reported to move unwanted staff into rooms with little or no work instead, hoping they quit. Japanese media call them oidashi beya, "chase-out rooms". In 2024 Bloomberg reported that a game studio had moved about 200 staff into such rooms and nearly 100 resigned; the company denied running any such room. Since 2020, Japan's power-harassment rules list isolating a worker as harassment, though there is no criminal penalty.

The characters and their story are a cartoon: the man in the corner and his empty desk are made up to show how it feels.
