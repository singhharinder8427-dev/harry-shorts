TITLE: Why can't you tickle yourself?
ALT TITLES: Your brain cancels your own tickle (here's how) | The reason only other people can tickle you

DESCRIPTION:
Same fingers, same spot, nothing happens. Your brain predicts your own touch and turns it down.

Every time you move, the brain uses a copy of the movement command to predict what you're about to feel. The cerebellum helps send that prediction ahead, so the touch you make yourself feels weaker. In a 1998 study at University College London, a robot touching people's palms in sync with their own movement barely tickled - but when the touch came about a fifth of a second late, it tickled more, and longer delays made it stronger.

The cartoon is exaggerated for fun. The robot study was small (six volunteers in the delay test), and scientists still debate some details of how this cancelling works.
