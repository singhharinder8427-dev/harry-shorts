Why can't you tickle yourself? Same fingers, same spot, and nothing happens. Your brain is one step ahead of you. Every time you move, it keeps a copy of the command and predicts exactly what you're about to feel. Your cerebellum sends that prediction ahead, and the touch is turned down before it arrives. Scientists in London tested it with robots. When people moved one robot arm and another touched their palm at the same moment, it hardly tickled. Add a delay of a fifth of a second, and the tickle came back. Your brain only lets you feel a tickle when it can't see it coming. Subscribe for more strange facts about your body.
