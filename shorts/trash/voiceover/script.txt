Throw something away, and it doesn't vanish. The town's trash piles into a stinking mountain. Burn it outside, and the sky turns black. So a crane drops it into a closed furnace, where it tumbles down a moving grate as air from below burns it. The heat boils water in pipes lining the walls. That steam spins a turbine for power, and leftover heat can warm homes. Lime neutralizes acid in the smoke, carbon traps mercury and dioxins, and fabric bags catch the dust. The white plume from the chimney is mostly water vapor. Magnets pull metal from the ash, and the rest can become road gravel. Subscribe to see how more everyday things work.
