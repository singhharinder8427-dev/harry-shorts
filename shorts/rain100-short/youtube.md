TITLE: What if it rained for 100 years straight?
ALT TITLES: What would 100 years of nonstop rain do to Earth? | The sky holds only one inch of water. So how does it keep raining?

DESCRIPTION:
What if it rained for a hundred years straight? The air only holds about one inch of water at a time.

The whole atmosphere carries only about one inch of water, and it falls out in about nine days. Earth recycles that water about forty times a year, roughly three and a half feet of rain. Do that for a century and it adds up to around 340 feet, if nothing drained away. Rain isn't a supply. It's a loop, and the Sun runs it.
