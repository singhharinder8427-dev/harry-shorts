## Variant 1

What happens if you stay awake for eleven days? In 1963, a seventeen-year-old named Randy Gardner tried it for a science fair. Two friends took turns keeping him awake. By day three, he felt sick and kept forgetting things. By day four, he was seeing things that weren't there. He even believed he was a famous football player, while weighing about a hundred and thirty pounds. A Stanford sleep scientist came to watch over him. And here is what a tired brain does. It doesn't wait for permission. It switches parts of itself off for a few seconds at a time, even with the eyes open. These are called microsleeps. After two hundred and sixty-four hours, Randy slept for about fourteen hours, and seemed fine. Guinness stopped tracking this record, because it's too dangerous.
