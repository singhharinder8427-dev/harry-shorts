# Script formula: beats scaled to the length the user asks for

Read this at section 1.2 / 1.4 of SKILL.md, after the user has given the length (`LIMIT`, section 0).
It is the skeleton every one of the five variants is built on. The angles in 1.4 change the *hook type and the
point of view*, not the skeleton. All rules of 1.2 still apply (no hype words, no "!", verified facts only,
one subscribe line at the end).

The beat order comes from a study of 20 successful story Shorts (hook as a paradox, escalation, a "you see" turn
with an everyday analogy, an ironic payoff). The numbers here are ours: our pace, our subscribe line, our lengths.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 1. Word budget from the length

- Speech seconds = `LIMIT` - 2.0 (0.4 s lead-in + 1.6 s tail, as in section 0).
- Total words = 2.7 x speech seconds (our voice pace, about 2.7 words/s).
- Subscribe line = about 9 words (~3 s). **Story words = total words - 9.**
- Seconds for any beat = its words / 2.7.

| LIMIT | Total words | Story words | Escalation beats | Mystery beat | Deadpan double line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 s | ~75 | ~66 | 2 | skip | skip |
| 45 s | ~115 | ~106 | 3 | 1 | optional |
| 55 s | ~145 | ~136 | 4 | 1 | optional |
| 60 s | ~157 | ~148 | 4 | 1 | optional |
| 90 s | ~238 | ~229 | 5-6 | 2 | optional |

Any other length: story words = round(2.7 x (`LIMIT` - 2)) - 9. Under 40 s use 2 escalation beats and no mystery
beat; 40-70 s use 3-4; over 70 s use 5-6 and a second mystery beat.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 2. The beats and how many words each gets

Approximate words per beat (they add up to the story words). Stay within about ±15 % per beat. The hook is the
one beat that does not grow with length: keep it 12-16 words at every length.

| # | Beat | 30 s | 45 s | 55 s | 60 s | 90 s | Share for other lengths |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 12 | 13 | 14 | 14 | 15 | 12-16 words, fixed |
| 2 | Setting | 6 | 10 | 14 | 15 | 22 | ~10 % |
| 3 | Escalation (all beats together) | 22 | 36 | 46 | 50 | 80 | ~35 % |
| 4 | Mystery / turn question | - | 5 | 6 | 7 | 14 | ~5 % |
| 5 | Pivot + answer (the why) | 18 | 28 | 36 | 40 | 60 | ~27 % |
| 6 | Consequence | (in payoff) | (in payoff) | (in payoff) | (in payoff) | 20 | only over 70 s |
| 7 | Payoff / callback ending | 8 | 14 | 20 | 22 | 18 | ~13 % |
| + | Subscribe line | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | fixed |

At 30 s the setting can be folded into the hook ("It's 1831, and seventy-four soldiers ..."). The payoff must not
be cut to save words: cut an escalation beat first, then the mystery beat, then setting detail.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 3. Writing each beat

### 1. Hook (sentence 1, no setup, 12-16 words)
State the strange part as a plain fact. The viewer must need the answer. Pick one pattern:
- **Absurd act with stakes:** someone did something that sounds dumb or impossible, and it mattered.
  "Seventy-four soldiers knocked down an iron bridge just by walking across it."
- **If you X, you'd Y:** a direct consequence for the viewer.
  "If every drop of water on Earth vanished, you wouldn't survive it."
- **Rule vs reality:** "X is illegal / forbidden / impossible, yet ..."
- **Imagine you're ...:** second person, dropped into the situation.
  "Stuck on a beach with only one tree, you'd sit under it. Don't."
- **Same start, different end:** two people with the same start and very different results (the chicken Short).

The hook line and the frame-0 flash-forward (section 3 of SKILL.md) show the same thing: the end state at its most
striking. Write the hook so that picture is obvious.

### 2. Setting (1-2 short lines)
Present tense, concrete: a year or a time of day, a place, one named everyday person or a role (Ray, Blob, "a
farmer"), one number. No backstory beyond what the story needs.

### 3. Escalation (2-6 beats, one sentence or two each)
Each beat is a little bigger than the last. Good engines:
- someone laughs, doubts or ignores the warning, and the stakes go up;
- the same action repeated harder ("a little harder every step");
- one problem solved creates a bigger one.
Put 1-2 jokes here. Jokes must be **real researched facts** (claim vs reality, a true absurd detail). Each beat must
be easy to show as one event (section 3: an event on every caption phrase).

### 4. Mystery / turn question (one short line)
The question the viewer is now asking, said out loud: "So why did a bridge built for carts fall for a few men?" /
"Wait, how does a rooster beat a hen?" Skip it at 30 s.

### 5. Pivot + answer (the why)
Open with a short pivot so the voice turns: "You see," (the most common in the study), "The reason is ...", "It comes
down to ...". Vary it per video. "Here's the thing" / "but here's the part" stay banned (1.2).
Then the real mechanism through **one everyday analogy** the viewer has felt:
"Ever pushed a kid on a swing? Each push is tiny, but if they all land at just the right moment, the swing goes
higher and higher." Patterns: "Ever ...?", "Think about the last time you ...", "Picture your body as ...".
One idea only. No jargon unless the next clause explains it. This is where the side-by-side "why" shot goes.

### 6. Consequence (only over 70 s; otherwise one clause inside the payoff)
What changed because of it: the rule, the law, the habit, the number today.

### 7. Payoff / callback ending (1-2 short sentences)
Ironic, short, lands the twist. The best ones call back to the hook or an escalation line:
- **Callback twist:** "So now, the best drilled soldiers in the world cross every bridge like a crowd of tourists."
- **Deadpan double line** (optional, 45 s and longer, where a fact really is two-sided): the same fact read as good
  and bad news. "Luckily, the river was only two feet deep. Unluckily, the river was only two feet deep."
  Use at most once per video, and only when the fact is true both ways.
- **Character reversal:** the character says or does the opposite of their first line ("Okay," Ray says, "I miss
  traffic.").
- **Three-way contrast:** "Same start. Same chicken. Now one is poor, one is comfortable, and one is rich."
The last story line should set up the loop back to frame 0 (the finale picture = the flash-forward picture).

### + Subscribe line (last, ~9 words)
One warm sentence with a reason, as in 1.2. It comes after the payoff, never in its place.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 4. Sentences and voice
- Median sentence ~13 words; mix in very short ones for punch ("Also his last.").
- Spoken English, contractions, present tense for the story.
- Never describe what the picture already shows ("as you can see"). Say the fact; the cast shows it.
- Two modes, both fine: story (third person, a named everyday person) or "Imagine you're ..." (second person).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 5. How the five angles (1.4) use the skeleton
| Angle | Hook pattern | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Curious / what-if | If you X, you'd Y | escalation = consequences in order |
| 2. Emotional / human | Imagine you're ... / a person's stakes | escalation through one person's feeling |
| 3. Story with characters | Same start, different end / absurd act | two characters, narrator only; the fact is the turn |
| 4. Myth-buster | Rule vs reality / what most people believe | mystery beat = "so why is everyone wrong?" |
| 5. Stakes / scale | the most striking number as an absurd fact | each escalation beat raises the number |

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 6. Check before showing a variant
- [ ] Word count within ±5 % of the total for `LIMIT` (table 1); estimated length = words / 2.7 + 2.0 s ≤ `LIMIT`.
- [ ] Hook is sentence 1, 12-16 words, a fact, no question-bait like "Did you know?".
- [ ] Escalation beats counted as in table 1, each bigger than the last, each one showable event.
- [ ] Exactly one analogy in the answer; the answer comes near the end, not in the first half.
- [ ] Payoff is 1-2 short sentences and ties back to the hook or an earlier line.
- [ ] Every joke and number is on the fact sheet.
- [ ] One subscribe line, last.
- [ ] No hype words, no "!", no banned filler (1.2).
Show the beat labels and the word count per beat next to each variant, so the user can see the structure.
